# Dream Course — Outline

Evidence review: [ASSESSMENT.md](ASSESSMENT.md). Its eleven proposed additions were applied here on 2026-10-12.

Draft v0.1, 2026-10-12. Seven modules, one every two weeks, plus a welcome module and
a maintenance phase. Each module is one 1.5–2.5 h session with a Dream Friend or, for
the solo parts, an evening alone with the Dream Book.

Roles are assignable (REQUIREMENTS D2). Two roles, Dreamer and Dream Friend, in three
configurations: **Solo + Friend** (one Dreamer, a Dream Friend who is not doing the
course), **Peer pair** (two Dreamers who alternate as Dream Friend), **Friend + group**
(one Dream Friend, 3–8 Dreamers). Every "With your Dream Friend" block below is written
for Solo + Friend and then gives the *Pair* and *Group* variants in one line each. In
the text, "the Friend" is whoever holds the Dream Friend role in that session.

The order follows the pilot but gives each CHILD / COPIL letter its own home, which one
pilot participant explicitly asked for in the closing survey. Deviations from the pilot
are marked "changed".

Legend per module: **Learn** (short reading in the app), **Do** (exercise, in the
Dream Book or the app), **With your Dream Friend** (pair exercise or 1:1),
**Until next time** (one practice, at most two, always written as an if-then plan:
"if [situation], then I will [action]"), **Takeaway** (the one sentence),
**Sources** (which pilot material it comes from).

---

## Module 0 — Welcome (solo, 30 min)

**Learn.** Survival mode: "somewhere between meetings, deadlines and delivery, your own
dreams got quieter." Dreams are the visions that shape our lives; when we stop
dreaming we disengage from work, relationships and life (Kelly). You do not need a big
dream; you learn here how to deal with a dream.

**Do.** Get a paper Dream Book (any notebook) and a pen. Print the Dream Book sheets
from the app or decide to write freehand. Set the start date and the interface
language (content is in Romanian).

**Ground rules** (from the kick-off deck, rewritten for friends): openness and
curiosity; be present during sessions; honesty, respect and confidentiality, what is
said stays between us; sharing is caring but never mandatory.

**With your Dream Friend.** Schedule the seven sessions, two weeks apart. *Pair:* agree
who opens as Friend in module 1 and alternate from there. *Group:* fix the seven dates
for everyone and agree the ground rules aloud.

**Takeaway.** This is about you as a whole person, not just your role.

**Sources.** DM_Pilot_Intro_Apr2026.pdf, Kick-off slides 3, 6.

## Module 1 — Dream again (C: start the list)

**Learn.** The CHILD / COPIL method in one picture: Clarify (Clarificare), Hurdles
(Obstacole), Inch by inch (Pași mici), Loyal friends (Inima bună), Diplomacy of the
mind (Liniște / Limite). Mental imagery and how to visualise better. Seneca: if one
does not know to which port one is sailing, no wind is favourable.

**Do.**
1. 100 dreams list in the Dream Book: dream without limits, date each dream as you add
   it, date it again when you achieve it. No deadline for reaching 100.
2. Kelly's twelve life areas as a prompt when stuck: physical, emotional,
   intellectual, spiritual, psychological, material, professional, financial,
   creative, adventure, legacy, character.
3. Thirty minutes a week of introspection, put in the calendar.

**With your Dream Friend.** Introductions the pilot way: one personal trait starting
with your initial, one childhood dream. Then the Friend shares three dreams from their
own list first. *Pair:* both share three dreams. *Group:* a round of introductions, then
the Friend shares first, then whoever wants to.

**Until next time.** Keep adding dreams. Watch the BJ Fogg TEDx talk or the Oettingen
interview if curious (links, optional).

**Takeaway.** Be a CHILD with your dreams: they need your childhood attitude.

**Sources.** Kick-off slides 7–9, 14–16; session 1 notes (resources list).

## Module 2 — Clarify one dream (C)

**Learn.** Dreams are truly dreams when they are important to us. Rating on your own
criteria; the one mandatory criterion is "how do I feel about this dream". The
five-step imagery table: Clarity (what does it look like in real life), Imagine (if
this were already true, what would I see around me), Feel (what emotions am I
experiencing), Anchor (why does this dream matter to who I am), Act (what is one small
step I can take now).

**Do.**
1. Rate every dream 1–10 and tag each as intrinsic (growth, relationships,
   contribution, health) or extrinsic (money, image, status). Prioritise. If the top
   of the list is all extrinsic, pause before choosing. Pick one dream to work on
   through the course.
2. Clarification sheet for that dream: describe it until it is almost tangible.
3. The four categories exercise (changed: came from a participant remark in session 2;
   kept as an optional way to sort the list before rating).
4. Three routes: three different ways this dream could be reached, so the plan never
   hangs on a single path.

**With your Dream Friend.** Walk the chosen dream through C: the Friend asks
clarification questions only, no advice. Then switch: the Dreamer plays Dream Friend
for one of the Friend's dreams. Debrief: how did each role feel. (The pilot only had
time for one direction and recommended switching; here both directions happen.)
*Pair:* this is the natural pair exercise, both dreams are real. *Group:* one dream
walked in front of everyone as a demonstration, then pairs do both directions.

**1:1 script A (30 min), Dream Friend section.** *Pair:* run once per person, same
meeting or two meetings. *Group:* optional, booked per Dreamer. Open with "tell me the dream as if it
has happened". Clarification questions: where are you, who is with you, what do you
see, what changed for you, what is the first thing you notice. Mirror back in the
learner's words. Close with: what one detail became clearer today.

**Until next time.** Write the clarified dream on one page of the Dream Book, dated.

**Takeaway.** A dream you can see is a dream you can plan.

**Sources.** Session 2 notes, Kick-off slide 9, Session 3 slide 3.

## Module 3 — Make room for it, then face the hurdle (H)

**Learn.** Eisenhower's matrix: important / urgent, Do now, Plan, Delegate, Eliminate.
Dream work lives in "important, not urgent" and only happens when planned. Mental
contrasting (Oettingen): positive thinking alone lowers energy; contrasting the wish
with the obstacle releases it. WOOP: Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan.

**Do.**
1. Eisenhower on a real week: write 5–8 tasks, place them, see where your time goes,
   move one thing to Plan this week, eliminate one.
2. WOOP sheet for the chosen dream: the main internal or external obstacle, and the
   "if obstacle, then I will" plan. The pilot's observation: once named, the obstacle
   does not look that scary, and there may be an opportunity in it.

**With your Dream Friend.** Share the matrix, then the WOOP. The Friend's job is to
keep the obstacle concrete and to ask "what would you do the moment it shows up".
*Pair:* both share, each is Friend for the other's WOOP. *Group:* matrices shared in
a round, WOOPs in pairs, one obstacle per pair reported back.

**Until next time.** Take the two free assessments and bring the results next time:
the Positive Intelligence saboteurs test and the VIA character strengths survey
(links only). Both take about 15 minutes.

**Takeaway.** Eisenhower is not about doing more, it is about doing what matters.

**Sources.** Session 3 deck and notes, Kick-off slide 10.

## Module 4 — Small steps, loyal friends, and fear (I, L)

**Learn.**
- Inch by inch: big step, big fear, no cortex access, failure; small step, small fear,
  cortex access, success (kick-off slide 11, after Maurer's Kaizen). Tiny Habits
  recipe: after I [anchor], I will [tiny behaviour], then I celebrate.
- Loyal friends: telling someone about a goal brings support, effort and progress,
  and telling someone you look up to raises commitment. One caveat: when others
  acknowledge who you are becoming, effort can drop, so share what you did this week,
  not the identity you aspire to. Roles: friend of the dream, accountability partner,
  someone you ask for help. Barbara Sher on isolation as the dream killer.
- Fear and courage (reconstructed from session 4 notes, no deck existed): we all have
  fears and that is not bad, fear moves us forward unless it freezes us; courage is
  not the opposite of fear but is born from it; "good enough" deserves more value than
  perfect, and deciding what is good enough is itself a skill. One reading pointer to
  Atlas of the Heart on fear, vulnerability and perfectionism.

**Do.**
1. One tiny habit toward the dream, designed with the recipe and started today.
   Expect two to five months before it runs on its own; it varies a lot per person.
2. Loyal friends map: three names, three roles, one concrete ask written as a sentence
   you will actually say.
3. Fear page: what is the fear behind the hurdle from module 3, and what would "good
   enough" look like.

**With your Dream Friend.** Role play the ask: the Dreamer asks the Friend for the
support they wrote down, as if the Friend were that person. Then the Friend becomes
the first loyal friend officially and agrees on how progress is reported (a weekly
one-line message is enough). *Pair:* both asks are rehearsed and each becomes the
other's loyal friend. *Group:* asks rehearsed in pairs; loyal-friend pairings are chosen
freely, not assigned.

**Until next time.** Report the tiny habit once a week in one line. Bring the saboteur
and VIA results.

**Takeaway.** Don't wait to feel ready; take the next step and let progress build
confidence.

**Sources.** Kick-off slides 11–12, session 1 notes (I and L resources), session 4
notes, Atlas of the Heart contents.

## Module 5 — Diplomacy of the mind (D)

**Learn.** The Judge, the master saboteur, and the nine accomplices: Hyper-Vigilant,
Hyper-Achiever, Controller, Stickler, Victim, Hyper-Rational, Pleaser, Restless,
Avoider (Chamine): a coaching vocabulary, not a diagnosis. You do not argue with
saboteurs; you let the Sage take the conversation, which is a self-compassion move:
speak to yourself as you would to a friend in the same spot. The Sage's five powers:
Empathize, Explore, Innovate, Navigate, Activate. Mental fitness: shifting from
saboteur to Sage is a trained reflex, built by intercepting the saboteur and doing a
PQ rep. Also the pilot's participant insight: the sage exercise is the empty chair
technique, you versus you, and not every thought is a truth.

**Do.**
1. Enter your top three saboteur scores and your top five VIA strengths.
2. Catch the saboteur: a recent situation that frustrated you. What happened, what did
   you think, what did you feel, what did you do, which saboteur was active, what would
   the Sage say. Keep this as a journal for the next two weeks.
3. PQ reps: three tactile reps a day for two weeks, timer in the app.
4. Wheel of Life: rate the eight areas, draw the new edge, ask how bumpy the ride is.
   Beside it, the five-item life satisfaction scale, so change can be seen at check-ins.
5. One signature strength in a new way, each day for a week, pointed at the dream.

**With your Dream Friend.** Saboteur versus Sage as a two-chair exercise. Round 1:
the Dreamer tells the situation and names the saboteur; the Friend voices the saboteur
out loud from one chair, then the Sage from the other. Round 2: switch roles with one
of the Friend's situations. Debrief: what was different in the body, not just in the
head. *Pair:* as written, both situations real. *Group:* "catch the saboteur" shared
in pairs first, then the two-chair rounds in the same pairs, then a group debrief.

**1:1 script B (60 min), Dream Friend section.** *Pair:* once per person. *Group:*
optional. Saboteur results and what surprised
the learner; strengths and where they already show up in the dream; the Wheel of Life
and which area, if raised by one point, would help the dream most; how the tiny habit
is going.

**Until next time.** Journal and reps. Optional reading: Positive Intelligence
chapters 1–8 (own copy), the Sage powers games.

**Takeaway.** You cannot shift to Sage unless you interrupt the saboteur first. Be as
kind with yourself as you are with others.

**Sources.** Session 4 and 5 notes, Session 5 deck, Sage Powers handout, saboteurs
reference, Wheel of Life sheet.

## Module 6 — Dream Vision, part 1

**Learn.** A dream vision is to your life what a product vision is to a product:
target customer becomes current and future self; customer problem becomes the gap
between how life is and how you aspire it to be; value proposition becomes your
contribution and impact; market landscape becomes life paths and environments; future
state becomes ideal life and identity; differentiation becomes your uniqueness. Simple
template: "I am a person who [identity], creating [impact] while living [lifestyle]".
The steps: understand your inner problem space, define your core self, explore the
life landscape, clarify your value proposition, define the desired future, identify
emotional drivers, draft the statement, reality check, translate into direction,
embed in daily life.

**Do.**
1. Values: list ten, narrow to five, mark the non-negotiables.
2. Letter from your future self, 10–15 years ahead (never less than 5): where are
   you, what are you doing, how do you feel, what have you built, what did you stop
   doing, what mattered most, what advice would future-you give. Make it vivid and the
   same person, older, not a wish list. Then write the reply from that future self.
   Seal both with a delivery date. The pilot's insight: the letter reveals the values better than the
   list did; check how many of the ten values appear in the letter.
3. Start the Dream Vision worksheet (sections 1–4: values, at my best, energy check,
   ten years ahead).

**With your Dream Friend.** Read a chosen part of the letter aloud, or describe it.
Reflection question for both: are the values that matter most now going to be the
same in 10–15 years? *Pair:* both read. *Group:* in pairs, then one sentence each in
the round; nobody is asked to read aloud to the group.

**Until next time.** Finish the worksheet sections 5–7 (impact, ideal day, recognition
and identity). Bring it to the 1:1.

**Takeaway.** Will the values that matter most to you now be the same in 15 years?

**Sources.** Session 6 deck and notes, Dream Vision worksheet.

## Module 7 — Dream Vision, part 2, and closing

**Learn.** The vision should maximise energy sources, not status. Tools map for
keeping a vision alive: clarity tools (values, energy mapping, peak experiences),
future-oriented tools (ideal day, ten-year letter, regret minimization), direction
tools (life domains canvas, three to five strategic pillars, identity-based vision),
synthesis tools (statement template, decision filter), maintenance tools (vision
check-in every 3 or 6 months, in the calendar).

**Do.**
1. Regret minimization: I am 75 and look back; what would I regret not having pursued,
   in impact, relationships, growth, courage.
2. Say Yes / Say No: in my dream future I say yes to, I say no to. What changes would
   be required, what feels hardest to let go of.
3. Finish the worksheet: the vision statement and three focus areas. Quick check: does
   it feel mine, does it stretch me a bit, would it help me say yes or no to an
   opportunity.
4. Letting go with honour: signs that a dream has become a tug-of-war (persist or
   quit, back and forth), a short cost-benefit page, and a re-engagement prompt that
   sends you back to the dream list. Dropping a dream well is a skill, not a failure.
5. Schedule the first vision check-in, three or six months out, and the quarterly
   dream list revisit.

**With your Dream Friend.** Share the statement. Retrospective, the pilot's three
questions: one tool or exercise that was most impactful, one habit introduced that
made a positive change, one aha moment and a reflection around it. One-word feedback
for the whole course. Celebrate one dream achieved or advanced, however small.
*Pair:* both statements, both retrospectives. *Group:* statements in pairs, the three
questions as a round, one-word feedback collected on paper, a shared celebration.

**1:1 script C (45 min), Dream Friend section.** *Pair:* once per person. *Group:*
optional. Worksheet review, section by section,
asking only "is this yours or someone's expectation of you". Agree the check-in dates
and how the loyal-friend reporting continues after the course.

**Takeaway.** This boat has arrived at its destination; for the crew, this is just the
beginning.

**Sources.** Session 7 deck and notes, Closing deck, closing survey.

## Maintenance phase (after the course, indefinitely)

- Vision check-in every 3 or 6 months: reread the statement, redraw the Wheel of Life
  and compare with the last one, re-read and re-rate the dream list in the Dream Book,
  update focus areas, review the Yes/No filter, fill the five-item scale again, and use
  the letting-go sheet if a dream has become a tug-of-war. Thirty minutes, calendar
  invite from the app.
- The tiny-habit tracker and the weekly one-line report stay on through month three.
- Future-self letter re-delivered on its date.
- Loyal-friend reporting continues at whatever rhythm was agreed in module 4.
- Dreams achieved get their second date in the Dream Book.

## Dream Friend section (app)

Shown to whoever holds the Dream Friend role (REQUIREMENTS FR-06). Everything in it
exists in three variants: Solo + Friend, Peer pair, Friend + group.

- Session plan per module with timings (based on the pilot's agendas: check-in and
  energy level, recap, one theory block, one practice block in pairs, debrief,
  feedback and until-next-time). The group variant adds the check-in round, pair
  forming (rotate pairs across modules), the group debrief and the one-word round.
- Pair exercise scripts for both seats, modules 2, 4, 5, 6, with the *Pair* and *Group*
  variants from the module text.
- 1:1 scripts A, B, C, with the peer-pair rule: once per person, alternate who goes
  first.
- Peer-pair rules: alternate the Dream Friend role per session; the person holding it
  does their own exercises before the meeting, not during; both keep their own Dream
  Book and their own data.
- Powerful questions list (open, non-leading, future-oriented).
- The stuck-learner lens: is the block agency ("I can't") or pathways ("I see no
  route")? Agency gets a self-compassion tool, pathways gets the three-routes sheet.
- The pilot's lessons for a Dream Friend: no two people have the same pace; most people
  want to deal with the saboteurs early, so do not postpone D too long; keep homework to
  one thing; longer sessions with more pair time beat short weekly ones.
