# Dream Course — Evidence Assessment of the Pilot Materials

Draft v0.2, 2026-10-12. Written for Florin as course designer and facilitator.
Companion documents: [REQUIREMENTS.md](REQUIREMENTS.md), [COURSE-OUTLINE.md](COURSE-OUTLINE.md).

## 1. Summary

The pilot's materials claim that "scientific methods to support you follow your dreams
are already available and well fundamented". That is true for about half of the toolkit
and overstated for the other half. The strong core is: implementation intentions and
mental contrasting (WOOP), the Best Possible Self family of exercises (future-self
letter, ideal day), self-concordant and intrinsic goal selection, signature strengths,
self-compassion work against the inner critic, and the coaching relationship itself.
The weak or unvalidated parts are: the Gail Matthews goal-writing statistics, the
Positive Intelligence "PQ" claims and saboteur assessment, the Wheel of Life and the
Eisenhower matrix as instruments, Tiny Habits as a branded method, and the Dream
Manager book, which is a business parable.

None of the weak parts is harmful. They are good vocabulary and good practice
scaffolds. The course should keep them, but present them honestly as heuristics and
let the evidence-backed mechanisms underneath carry the weight. Section 6 lists eleven
concrete additions from the current state of the art that the pilot did not have.

## 2. Sourcing rule and method

Every claim in sections 3 to 6 rests on a peer-reviewed journal article listed in
section 8. Each entry there was resolved through Crossref on 2026-10-12 and carries
journal, year and DOI. Two entries are invited review series (Advances in Experimental
Social Psychology, Annual Review of Psychology); they are editorially reviewed rather
than conventionally peer-reviewed and are marked as such, and each is backed by a
peer-reviewed article making the same point.

Three sources that are not peer-reviewed appear in section 9 only, as the objects of
critique: the Matthews goal study summary, the Positive Intelligence company white
paper, and Mirela Oprea's own books and site. Nothing in the course should cite them as
evidence. Magazine articles, blogs, press releases, editorials and preprints found
during the search were discarded.

Limits: abstracts and meta-analysis summaries were read, not every full text. Effect
sizes are quoted as reported in abstracts. Search was web-based, not a systematic
database search, so relevant papers may have been missed.

## 3. Evidence grades used

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Meta-analytic support for the specific technique, or for a technique that is the same in all but name |
| B | At least one randomised or longitudinal peer-reviewed study of the specific technique, or strong theory with converging peer-reviewed evidence |
| C | Plausible, consistent with established theory, widely used in practice, no direct outcome evidence |
| D | No independent evidence, or the evidence offered is proprietary, anecdotal, or contradicted |

Effect sizes: d or g around 0.2 is small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large.

## 4. Method-by-method assessment

| Method in the pilot | Where | Grade | What the evidence says | What to do in the course |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 dreams list, Dream Book, dating dreams (Kelly) | M1 | C | No research on the practice itself. Goal setting in general has a small unique effect, d = 0.34 across 384 effect sizes, larger for difficult, public and group goals (Epton, Currie & Armitage 2017). Progress monitoring raises attainment, d = 0.40 across 138 studies, more when progress is physically recorded or made public (Harkin et al. 2016). | Keep. Present as a goal-generation and recording habit, not as a studied method. The "date it again when achieved" rule is a progress-monitoring device and deserves emphasis. |
| Rating dreams 1–10 with "how do I feel" as a criterion | M2 | A (indirect) | Self-concordant goals (pursued for interest and value, not pressure) are more sustainable and raise well-being across three longitudinal datasets (Sheldon & Elliot 1999). Intrinsic aspirations correlate with well-being (r ≈ 0.24); extrinsic aspirations that dominate correlate negatively (r ≈ −0.22) in a 105-study meta-analysis (Bradshaw et al. 2023). | Keep and strengthen: add an explicit "why do I want this" check that sorts a dream as intrinsic (growth, relationships, contribution, health) or extrinsic (money, image, status), and warn when the top of the list is all extrinsic. |
| Mental imagery, "result and process" | M1, M2 | B | Process simulation improves planning, lowers anxiety and improves grades; outcome-only simulation reduced study hours and expectations (Pham & Taylor 1999). | Keep the five-step table but make the "Act" and "steps and stages" rows the heavy part. Never leave the learner with outcome imagery alone. |
| Mental contrasting and WOOP (Oettingen) | M3 | A | Mental contrasting with implementation intentions: g = 0.34 across 21 studies, 15,907 participants, larger (g = 0.47) when delivered by a person rather than a document (Wang, Wang & Gai 2021). Mental contrasting for health behaviour: g = 0.28 (Cross & Sheffield 2019). Implementation intentions alone: d = 0.65 across 94 tests (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, invited review). One recent trial found MCII no better than other planning formats (Abdulla & Woods 2021). | Keep. Make the "Plan" step a proper if-then sentence and have the facilitator walk it, since the human-delivered variant is the stronger one. |
| Tiny Habits, Atomic Habits, Kaizen (small steps) | M4 | C for the brand, B for the mechanism | One three-arm RCT (n = 154) of a gratitude-themed Tiny Habits program raised gratitude for a month (Hollingsworth & Redden 2022); it does not isolate the recipe or the "celebration" step. Habit-formation interventions do increase habit strength, but habits take 2–5 months, median 59–66 days, range 4–335 days (Singh et al. 2024); physical-activity habit interventions have a small pooled effect and problem-solving is the technique most tied to success (Ma et al. 2023). | Keep the recipe as a practical format. Replace any "21 days" or "66 days" claim with "two to five months, and it varies a lot". Design the maintenance phase so the habit is still being tracked at month three. |
| Loyal friends, accountability partner (Matthews study) | M4 | B, but the cited study is D | The Matthews study is an unpublished university summary (see section 9); the popular "42% more likely" figure is not in it. Peer-reviewed evidence: being assigned to share a goal increased support received, effort and progress (Peetz, Buehler & Wells 2025); sharing with a higher-status audience increased commitment and performance across four studies (Klein et al. 2020); but having an identity goal acknowledged by others reduced effort through a premature sense of completeness (Gollwitzer, Sheeran, Michalski & Seifert 2009). | Keep the practice, drop the Matthews percentages. Teach the nuance: share with someone you respect, share actions and progress rather than the identity you aspire to, and report weekly. |
| Eisenhower matrix | M3 | C | Time management in general relates moderately to well-being and, less strongly, to performance (Aeon, Faber & Panaccio 2021). No peer-reviewed controlled test of the matrix itself was found. | Keep as a heuristic for making room; present it as such. |
| Positive Intelligence: saboteurs, Sage, PQ reps, PQ score (Chamine) | M5 | D for the claims, B for the mechanisms | The only peer-reviewed study of the program is a single-group longitudinal study of a six-week app with occupational therapy students, reporting lower perceived stress and rumination and higher self-compassion at five months (Puah et al. 2024); validation of the assessment is a company white paper (section 9). The mechanisms are well supported: self-compassion interventions reduce self-criticism, g = 0.51 across 19 studies (Wakelin, Perman & Simonds 2022); three sessions of emotion-focused chairwork with the inner critic reduced self-criticism and depression at one month in a seven-patient pilot (Kroener, Mahler & Sosic-Vasic 2024); affect labeling regulates emotion implicitly, comparably to reappraisal (Torre & Lieberman 2018); brief breath-awareness and loving-kindness practices buffered affect under an acute stressor in an RCT of 156 students (Hirshberg et al. 2018). | Keep the saboteur vocabulary and the Sage exercise, with attribution. Remove any PQ score, "75% tipping point", or sales and health statistics. Add one line that the names are a coaching model, not a diagnostic. Frame PQ reps as brief mindfulness and label the Sage dialogue as a self-compassion and chairwork exercise, which is where the evidence is. |
| VIA character strengths survey | M3, M5 | A | Signature strengths interventions: g = 0.32 for positive affect, 0.42 for life satisfaction, 0.21 for depression across 14 articles (Schutte & Malouff 2019). | Keep, and actually use the results: the pilot asked for the test but never built an exercise on it. Add "use a signature strength in a new way toward the dream this week", which is the studied intervention. |
| Wheel of Life | M5, maintenance | C | No peer-reviewed reliability or validity study of the Wheel of Life was found in the search. | Keep for reflection. For tracking change at check-ins, add a validated five-item scale beside it (Satisfaction With Life Scale, or WHO-5), both free. |
| Values: list 10, narrow to 5 | M6 | B | Self-affirmation interventions, including value affirmation, have small but significant effects on self-perception, general and social well-being, some of them delayed, across 129 tests (Zhang et al. 2025). An "activating values" RCT with 476 participants found that choosing a valued area, affirming it and acting on it within a week improved self-insight, sense of coherence and prioritising meaning (Russo-Netzer & Atad 2024). | Keep. Add the "act on one value within a week" step; the affirmation alone is the weaker half. |
| Letter from the future self, ideal day | M6 | A | The Best Possible Self family: d = 0.33 well-being, 0.33 optimism, 0.51 positive affect across 29 studies (Carrillo et al. 2019); a later meta-analysis finds smaller effects, g = 0.28 positive affect, 0.21 optimism (Heekerens & Eid 2021); BPS raised optimism more than other interventions (Malouff & Schutte 2017). Future self-continuity: writing to a 20-year-distant self increased exercise in the following days (Rutchick et al. 2018); a 2025 systematic review treats letter writing as a standard continuity intervention (Grekin et al. 2025); a letter-exchange with the future self improved self-continuity and career planning in students (Chishima & Wilson 2020). Feeling connected but dissimilar to the future self narrowed the intention-behaviour gap (PLOS ONE 2024). | Keep. Make the letter vivid and connected, not merely rosy. Add the letter-exchange variant (write to the future self, then reply as the future self). |
| Dream Vision statement, product vision analogy, focus areas | M6, M7 | C | A synthesis of possible-selves and self-concordance ideas. No test of a "vision statement" as such. Two pilot participants named the product-vision analogy as their aha moment. | Keep. Keep the analogy for engineers; offer a plain version for others. |
| Regret minimization at 75, Flash Forward | M7 | C | Anticipated regret predicts intentions (r = 0.50) and behaviour (r = 0.29) across 81 studies, and anticipated regret from not acting predicts stronger intentions than regret from acting (Brewer, DeFrank & Gilkey 2016). No study of the "look back from old age" exercise itself. | Keep as a decision tool; it is cheap and safe. |
| Say Yes / Say No decision filter | M7 | C | No direct evidence; consistent with the finding that fewer, more concordant goals are easier to sustain (Sheldon & Elliot 1999). | Keep. |
| Vision check-in every 3 or 6 months | Maintenance | B | Progress monitoring and review improve attainment (Harkin et al. 2016); expressive writing effects are larger when sessions are close together (Guo 2023); the handwriting advantage in note-taking, g = 0.25, appears only when notes are reviewed later (Flanigan et al. 2024). | Keep. This is the single most important habit; the app's reminder exists for it. Add a dream-list re-read to every check-in, since the handwriting benefit depends on re-reading. |
| Atlas of the Heart (Brené Brown) | M4 reading | B | Emotion differentiation is linked to better coping and regulation (Kashdan, Barrett & McKnight 2015); low differentiation goes with ineffective use of regulation strategies (Kalokerinos et al. 2019). Affect labeling reduces distress as well as reappraisal does, and the review notes weaker effects in a non-native language (Torre & Lieberman 2018). | Keep as a pointer. Design implication: have learners name feelings in their first language, which supports Romanian-first for the reflective exercises. |
| Fear and courage (session 4) | M4 | C | Courage is understood as a wilful, deliberate act toward a noble goal, involving substantial risk, carried out despite fear (Rate, Clarke, Lindsay & Sternberg 2007). "Good enough over perfect" matches the finding that maximisers report lower happiness and more regret than satisficers (Schwartz et al. 2002). | Keep, using Rate's components as the structure of the page. |
| The CHILD / COPIL method as a whole (Oprea) | All | C | A synthesis by a psychotherapist, published as a 2019 book; no peer-reviewed evaluation found (section 9). Its components map onto evidence-backed constructs, which is its strength. | Keep, attribute, and say in the Sources page that the method is a synthesis of studied components rather than a studied program. |
| The Dream Manager (Kelly) | M0, M1 | D as evidence, fine as inspiration | A business parable about employee retention; no research. | Use only for the narrative and the twelve life areas. |
| Group sessions, 1:1 sessions, Dream Friend | Structure | A | Coaching improves well-being (g = 0.46) and goal-directed self-regulation (g = 0.74) across 18 studies (Theeboom, Beersma & van Vianen 2014); workplace coaching overall d = 0.36 (Jones, Woods & Guillaume 2016); RCT-only executive coaching g = 0.43 overall, 0.73 for behaviours (Nicolau et al. 2023); psychologically informed coaching g = 1.29 on goal attainment and 0.59 on self-efficacy across 20 studies (Wang et al. 2022). Neither Jones et al. nor Nicolau et al. found that the number of sessions moderated effects. In one RCT (n = 84) individual coaching beat group training on goal attainment while group training was better for knowledge (Losch et al. 2016). Human coaches and an AI coach were equally effective in two 2022 trials (Terblanche et al. 2022), but accredited human coaches outperformed an AI chatbot on most outcomes in a 2026 RCT of 114 coachees (de Haan, Terblanche & Nowack 2026); a separate longitudinal RCT found an AI chatbot coach improved students' goal attainment versus controls (Ohlms et al. 2026). | Keep the human Dream Friend as the core. Pair work for knowledge, 1:1 for the dream itself, exactly as the pilot did. An AI partner stays out of v1. |

## 5. What the pilot got right, in evidence terms

1. **It used mental contrasting, not positive thinking.** The kick-off deck's WOOP slide and the "obstacle did not look that scary" debrief are the evidence-backed core.
2. **It made the dream self-concordant before planning it.** Rating by "how do I feel" and picking one dream is the self-concordance model applied without naming it.
3. **It wrote the future self.** The letter plus the ideal day are the two best-studied positive psychology exercises in the toolkit.
4. **It put a human in the loop.** Delivery by a person is the moderator that raises the WOOP effect, and coaching meta-analyses are consistently positive.
5. **It spread sessions out.** Session count does not moderate coaching outcomes, and participants asked for two-week spacing. The planned cadence is defensible.
6. **It ended with a maintenance habit.** The quarterly check-in is the only thing in the program that addresses the 2–5 month habit horizon.

What the pilot overstates: the Matthews percentages, the Positive Intelligence performance claims and PQ score, any fixed habit-duration number, and "well fundamented" applied to CHILD, Tiny Habits, Eisenhower or the Wheel of Life as packaged tools rather than to the mechanisms beneath them. The Dream Manager book is inspiration, not evidence.

## 6. Additional insights from the current state of the art (proposed additions)

Each item names the module where it would go and the requirement it touches.

1. **If-then plans as the default output of every module (M2–M7, FR-21).** The single strongest tool in the literature is the implementation intention (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006; Wang, Wang & Gai 2021). Every "until next time" should end as "If [situation], then I will [action]".

2. **Intrinsic versus extrinsic sort of the dream list (M2, FR-11).** One extra tag per dream. The meta-analytic finding that extrinsic dominance predicts lower well-being (Bradshaw et al. 2023) is the best argument the course has for choosing dreams by feeling rather than by status.

3. **Goal disengagement and action crisis (new, M7 or maintenance, new FR).** The pilot had no way to drop a dream well. The capacity to disengage from unattainable goals and re-engage with new ones predicts well-being (Wrosch et al. 2003) and buffers physical health effects (Wrosch et al. 2007); the action crisis is the recognisable phase where a dreamer oscillates between persisting and quitting (Brandstätter, Herrmann & Schüler 2013; Brandstätter & Bernecker 2022). Add a "letting go with honour" sheet: signs of an action crisis, a cost-benefit page, and a re-engagement prompt that sends the learner back to the dream list. The Goal Disengagement and Reengagement Scale from Wrosch et al. 2003 is short and free.

4. **Future-self continuity, not just positivity (M6, FR-41).** Write to a self 10–20 years away, make it vivid and connected (same person, older), then reply as that self (Rutchick et al. 2018; Chishima & Wilson 2020). Avoid pure wish-fulfilment, which the 2024 PLOS ONE study suggests may weaken the effect.

5. **Signature strength in a new way (M5, new FR).** The studied intervention is "use one of your top five strengths in a new way each day for a week" (Schutte & Malouff 2019). The pilot collected VIA results and never used them. Tie the strength to the dream.

6. **Share progress, not identity (M4, FR-23).** Teach the Gollwitzer et al. 2009 caveat explicitly: tell your loyal friend what you did this week, not who you are becoming. Choose a friend you look up to for the commitment effect (Klein et al. 2020).

7. **Self-compassion as the frame for the Sage (M5, FR-30–32).** The evidence sits under self-compassion (Wakelin et al. 2022) and chairwork (Kroener et al. 2024), not under PQ. Add one self-compassion exercise and keep the Sage dialogue as a two-chair exercise.

8. **Name emotions in the mother tongue (M4–M5, D4).** Affect labeling works implicitly, and the weaker effect in a non-native language reported by Torre & Lieberman 2018 suggests the reflective exercises should be done in Romanian even for a learner who uses English at work.

9. **A validated five-item well-being measure beside the Wheel of Life (M5, maintenance, FR-34).** Satisfaction With Life Scale or WHO-5 at the start, at the end, and at each check-in. The Wheel stays for reflection; the scale shows whether anything moved.

10. **Realistic habit horizon and a progress log (M4, maintenance, FR-22, FR-45).** Say "two to five months" (Singh et al. 2024) and keep the tiny-habit tracker alive through the maintenance phase, with the weekly one-line report to the loyal friend as the monitoring mechanism that Harkin et al. 2016 favour.

11. **Hope theory as the facilitator's model (facilitator section).** Hope-enhancement interventions have small effects on hopefulness, d = 0.22 across 27 studies, and none on distress (Weis & Speridakos 2011), so this is a lens, not a tool: when a learner is stuck, ask whether the problem is agency (belief they can) or pathways (routes they can see), and respond with a self-compassion or a planning tool accordingly. Add a "three alternative routes" prompt to the CHILD sheet.

Two ideas considered and not recommended for v1: starting on a temporal landmark conflicts with the decision to let the learner choose any start date, though the app could mention it; an AI conversation partner is unsupported by the most recent head-to-head trial (de Haan et al. 2026) and weakens the privacy stance.
